GOAL
Replace the 3D placeholder on /viewer-next with the real viewport. The panel from 4a stays
as it is. The old viewer stays untouched.

Read docs/viewer-spec.md first. Binding for this build: section 9 (what is drawn on the
model - the always-on dimension lines and the selection box), section 10 (measure line and
viewport controls, with the placement table in 10.4), section 11 (screen sizes), section 12
("the viewer never throws").

BUILD IT IN FOUR STEPS, IN THIS ORDER
You cannot see your own result: this preview has no WebGL context, so the 3D canvas stays
blank for you no matter how good the code is. Do not try to look at it, and never "fix" a
blank canvas - that blankness is the environment, not a bug. The four steps below exist so
that you can check your work anyway, by reading numbers and text instead of pixels.

STEP 1 - the geometry, as a pure function, no graphics
Write buildModel(measurement) in its own module. It takes a measurement and returns plain
data: for every wall, roof face, edge, opening and attachment its id, its corner
coordinates in mm, and its colour. No three.js, no DOM, no canvas - a function that could
run in a terminal. This is the one place where the house is worked out.
- Geometry comes from the measurement only, never from hard-coded shapes.
- Colour per face from faces[].color.hex, secondary_hex as a subtle accent where present;
  a face with no colour, or colour confidence low, comes back neutral grey.
- Degrade, never throw (spec 12). The fixture is a plain gable house, but real measurements
  carry roof_type "other", face classes you do not model and attachments you do not draw.
  Whatever you cannot build becomes a simple massing or is left out, and the function
  reports that in a list of notes - it never raises.

STEP 2 - tests, so you can see the numbers
These tests are how you find out whether your house is right. Run them before going on.
Test against three different inputs, not one - the fixture alone would let you tune the
code until that one house fits while every real measurement falls over.

a) The fixture, fixtures/garage-house.json - for exact numbers:
   - 6 roof faces, 6 walls, 20 openings, the garage included
   - every opening lies fully inside the face its parent_face_id names
   - overall width 40' 0" and eave height 18' 0", so a model at the wrong scale fails loudly

b) A real measurement from export-messungen/ - for robustness, not for exact numbers.
   These are messy on purpose: values are missing, many are estimated, the roof is not a
   plain gable. Assert only that a model comes back, that the parts you could not build are
   listed as notes, and that nothing throws. They are older v1.5 exports - if the type or
   the validator rejects them outright, say so in your report and write a reduced
   measurement by hand instead of forcing them through.

c) A minimal measurement you write yourself, carrying only what the schema actually
   requires: faces with id, face_class and area_mm2 - no widths, no heights, no gable, no
   colours, no openings. This is the shape a poor photo run produces. It must return a
   simple massing plus notes, in neutral grey, and must not throw.

STEP 3 - an SVG debug view, so you can read your own drawing
Add a route /viewer-next/debug.svg that takes the same buildModel output and projects it
flat - a front elevation and a side elevation, walls as outlines, openings as rectangles
inside them, roof faces outlined, each shape carrying its id. Plain SVG, no WebGL, no
library.
SVG is text: open it and read the coordinates back. A window that sits outside its wall, a
roof that floats above the walls, a garage in the wrong place - all of that is visible in
the numbers. Fix what the drawing shows before you touch three.js. Say in your report what
you found this way.

STEP 4 - the renderer
Only now three.js, and only as the painter: it takes buildModel's output and draws it. No
geometry decisions in this layer.
- Free orbit, the three controls exactly where spec 10.4 puts them per width.
- Windows, doors and garage doors are clickable in their own right, like faces and edges.
- Clicking an element opens the selection box (spec 9.2) and highlights the matching panel
  row; clicking a panel row highlights the element and orbits the camera to it.
- The three dimension lines (spec 9.1): width, ridge, eave height, always visible, part of
  the picture and not of the measure tool - clearing measure lines never removes them.
- Measure line with edge and corner snapping (spec 10.1).
- Selection stays visible on top of the real colours: outline or glow, never a fill swap.

REPORT

- the browser console, which must be free of errors apart from the missing WebGL context
- a short list of what I should check by eye in my own browser
